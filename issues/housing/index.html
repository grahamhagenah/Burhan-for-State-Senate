Policy: Housing

Affordability and Abundance

Every family should be able to afford to put down roots in the city they choose.

As Cambridge City Councilor, I helped steer Cambridge towards tackling the housing shortage. We passed a slew of new policies that are bringing down rent prices and creating new homes for thousands of families, who don't have to choose between an affordable mortgage and the city they love.

It's time we make this life possible for the entire state.

The problem

Why Housing Got This Expensive

The Last 100 Years

For the last 100 years, it was illegal to build a triple-decker anywhere in Massachusetts.

Immigrants, people of color, and lower-income families moved to the cities in search of a better life. Boston and Somerville's populations were greater in the 1930s than they are today. Meanwhile, wealthier whites panicked at having to share the neighborhood with them. They enacted zoning policies that restricted building denser, cheaper housing, ensuring that only the "right" kind of people could live in the neighborhoods they chose.

Literally. Cambridge's zoning code explicitly stated they only wanted millionaires to live there. So I wrote the reforms that cut that part out.

It's beyond time to change all of these exclusionary policies on the state level.

Photo: Cambridge triple-deckers

The Austin Benchmark

10 years ago, Austin, Texas was facing a housing crisis. New jobs and the city's reputation were drawing in more people, but there wasn't enough housing for everyone. From 2010 to 2019, its rents were surging by 93%.

Beginning in 2015, Austin instituted new policies aimed at making it easier to build housing. It changed zoning to allow construction of large apartment buildings. It invested $250 million into affordable housing. And it sped up permitting for those who wanted to build.

The reforms worked. After increasing housing by 30%, Austin's rents started to fall, and they've continued to fall year after year.

Photo: Austin, TX apartment construction

The National Picture

On average, the population within blue states is decreasing, while red states are increasing in numbers. Why? Because red states are building housing.

Because of these changes, California is likely to lose four congressional seats by 2030, while Texas is set to gain four. In our political system, people are power.

Massachusetts already lost one congressional seat in 2010. With the Supreme Court allowing partisan gerrymandering and the decreasing political power of blue states, building more housing allows us to regain affordability and reclaim our democracy at the same time.

The plan

How We Will Create More Housing

Idea 01

Uniform zoning

Enact statewide zoning reform. We need one single statewide standard that will make building housing easy and predictable in every municipality. If you can build something in Cambridge, you can build it in Winchester. This doesn't have to be radical — Beacon Hill is already thinking of allowing duplexes everywhere. That's a start. I'll make sure we keep going.

Idea 02

Reform the permitting process

Right now, it takes months to get approvals, and there are multiple hoops to jump through. We should speed up the process and make it less confusing.

Idea 03

Build affordable housing

In Cambridge, I updated the Affordable Housing Overlay, which made it easier to build 100% affordable housing. We need to fund and prioritize affordable housing so that everyone can stay here, regardless of income level.

Idea 04

Single-stair reform

We are losing housing because of outdated fire codes that haven't been proven to make us safer. Larger buildings need to have two stairwells, not one — that takes up square footage that could be someone's home. Right now, we're "studying" this measure. I'll make sure it passes.

Idea 05

Denser housing near transit hubs

Regardless of where you live, we can agree that an apartment building near an MBTA station just makes sense. As State Senator, I'll push the MBTA to develop housing near Alewife and other transit hubs.

Idea 06

Land value tax

Right now, if you do nothing with the land you own, you pay nothing while it increases in value. That leads to empty storefronts and vacant buildings. We should tax the value of the land, not what's on it.

The missed opportunity

Alewife Should Already Be Full of Housing

Alewife Station is the perfect place to build denser housing. So why does nobody want to? The MBTA sent out the worst request for proposal I've ever seen. It was vague and unfocused and lacking in vision. When nobody bid on it, the MBTA claimed that market conditions were unfavorable. That's not true.

As State Senator, I'll push the MBTA to develop housing near Alewife and other transit hubs.

I founded Abundant Housing Massachusetts and the Welcoming Neighbors Network. I pushed for the starter homes ballot initiative and the ROAD to Housing Act.

I've been endorsed by Somerville YIMBY, YIMBY Action, and [endorser name pending].

People told me it couldn't be done. They said it was impossible, especially in Cambridge. I refused to take no for an answer. And now, Cambridge's Affordable Housing Overlay is being replicated across the country. Its multifamily housing zoning made international news.

Massachusetts is the first in a lot of things: the most educated state, the best public school system, and best state economy. But we are dead last in housing production. It's time for Massachusetts to lead the way.
